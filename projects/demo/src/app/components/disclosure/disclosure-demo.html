Disclosure

Also known as details, expansion panel, collapsible, accordion item.

A disclosure shows and hides the content after its summary. It is the native <details>. The browser opens it with a click, Enter or Space, tells screen readers whether it is expanded, and opens it when find-in-page matches text inside. Interop draws the chevron and the spacing, and eases the height open where the browser can. It needs no script.

Use one for content only some readers need. Content most readers need belongs on the page: GOV.UK found that some people never open it. Several in a row are an accordion.

A note to look into

Word the summary as the reader's question, or as what it shows. Write it as text: a link or a button inside a summary is a control inside a control, and the dev audit warns.

Open at first

open starts it open. In Angular, bind [open] and listen for (toggle).

A row

interop-disclosure="row" is a full-width row with a hover fill and a hairline above and below. Rows stack into an accordion.

What it does not do

It has no disabled state: a summary that cannot open is text, so write it as text. Do not give the summary a role: Scott O'Hara found that Safari on macOS then drops its expanded state. Do not use it for a menu, tabs or a dialog; Adrian Roselli explains why. A closed disclosure is left out when the page prints.

Callout

Also known as admonition, inline alert, message bar, inset text.

A callout sets a short note apart from the text around it: a warning before a destructive step, a tip, a fact the reader should not miss. It is always in the page and says nothing when it appears. It is a <div role="note"> that you write, so the heading level and the wording are yours. It needs no script.

Use it sparingly and next to what it qualifies. Readers learn to skip boxes that are everywhere: GitHub suggests one or two per article, never back to back, and GOV.UK found that some users do not notice inset text.

A warning

The icon, the word in small print and the edge color each show the status. Color alone would not reach a screen reader, or anyone who cannot tell the colors apart.

Statuses

itx-color is info, success, warning or danger, the same values as the toast and the badge, in the same colors. Without it, the callout is a neutral note. Each status has its Tabler icon; leave the icon out of a neutral note if it adds nothing.

Saying the status

Write the status word in the callout. Show it in small print with <small>, or hide it with itx-visually-hidden, the way GOV.UK's warning text does.

Visible or hidden?

Visible: every reader sees the word, including people who cannot tell the colors apart and people using forced colors, where every edge turns one color.

Hidden: a screen reader still hears it, but sighted readers rely on the icon. Keep the icon; the dev audit warns when a callout has neither a visible word nor an icon.

A title

Use a heading when the callout is a part of the page a reader might jump to, at the level that fits the outline: a callout cannot know it. Point aria-labelledby at the heading to name the note; some screen readers announce a note only when it has a name.

A plain note

A note with no status needs no icon and no status word.

Why there is no close button

A callout explains something about the content beside it, so it stays as long as that content does. Carbon's callout cannot be dismissed for the same reason, and GOV.UK has not found that dismissing banners helps. A close button also moves focus: once the box is gone, the button that had focus is gone with it. A message that the reader should acknowledge and clear is a toast.

Not for messages that appear

A callout never takes role="alert" or role="status". Screen readers do not announce an alert that is in the page when it loads, and one that a route inserts interrupts the reader on every visit. To say that something happened, use a toast or announce(). Research: Adrian Roselli on the note role.

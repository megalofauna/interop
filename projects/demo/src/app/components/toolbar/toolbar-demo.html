Toolbar

Also known as command bar, action group.

A toolbar holds three or more controls that act on one thing, such as the formatting buttons of a comment box. It is one Tab stop, and the arrow keys move between its controls, so a keyboard user reaches the text area after it in one press. You write the markup: role="toolbar", focusgroup="toolbar" and a name. Browsers with focusgroup move focus themselves. Elsewhere the InteropToolbar directive from interop/toolbar, or bindToolbar(root) from interop, does it.

One or two buttons, or a form's Save and Cancel, are not a toolbar. Leave them as buttons, where Tab reaches each one.

@for (group of groups; track group.label) {
@for (action of group.actions; track action.label) { {{ action.label }} }
}

A comment box

Bold, Italic, Strikethrough and Code turn on and off. With nothing selected, Bold starts bold text where you type; press it again at the end of the bold text to carry on in plain text. Each shows as pressed while the cursor is in its markers. Heading, Quote and the lists turn a line's prefix on and off. Link is a command. Focus returns to the comment after each, and Ctrl+Z or Cmd+Z undoes the change.

Keyboard

Key Does
Tab, Shift+Tab Enters and leaves the toolbar. Coming back lands on the last button used.
Left, Right Move between buttons, across the groups. Right to left flips them. Add wrap to focusgroup to wrap at the ends.
Home, End Go to the first and last button.
Enter, Space Press the button.

What goes inside

Buttons, and named role="group" elements around related buttons. A screen reader announces the group's name as focus enters it, and the toolbar spaces groups apart. A disabled button is skipped by the arrow keys; one with aria-disabled="true" is not, so it can still say what it does.

A text field and radio buttons use the arrow keys themselves, so focus cannot leave them with the arrows. Put at most one text field in a toolbar, last. For one choice of several, use buttons with aria-pressed, or a segmented control beside the toolbar.

For a vertical toolbar, write focusgroup="toolbar block" and aria-orientation="vertical". The browser does not announce the orientation from the token.

Make it yours

A toolbar's look is plain CSS, at three levels. Pick a look Interop ships with a word in the attribute. Tune any look with tokens. Or write your own with a class. Every Interop rule has zero specificity, so a single class wins over it, with no !important and no layers.

itx-size on the toolbar sizes every button in it. A button's own itx-size inside it is ignored, and the console says so.

Channel

interop-toolbar="channel" sets the buttons in a filled channel, the segmented control's. They show no fill or edge until hovered, and a bar separates the groups. Pressed, disabled, focus and forced colors are handled.

A look of your own

A class on the toolbar, and CSS on the same hooks Interop's own looks use: the toolbar, its groups, and the buttons in it. Set --itx-toolbar-* on the toolbar and --itx-button-* on the buttons. A button declares its own tokens, so setting --itx-button-* on the toolbar does not reach it.

Keep the attribute's values for Interop's looks, and name yours with a class, so a look Interop adds later cannot collide with it.

What a look must keep

Interop's looks are tested for these. A look of your own has to keep them itself.

The focus ring The toolbar is one Tab stop. The ring shows which button has it.
A pressed state that is more than color A toggle such as Bold must read as on without color vision (WCAG 1.4.1). The button doubles its edge.
Text and icons at 4.5:1 against what is behind them A button with no fill sits on the toolbar's background (WCAG 1.4.3).

Surfaces stay outside this namespace, and they are two things rather than one. --itx-surface-1 through -6 are values, one sequence. --itx-surface, --itx-surface-above and --itx-surface-below are pointers into it, written by the engine from depth — a source rather than an override point.

That is the whole substrate vocabulary in one word. page and layer were two names for one ramp, and layer in particular collided with the itx-layer attribute, which already means depth. The grouping survives as a spacing property documented beside the values: 1 to 3 are the ground, 3% apart, and 4 to 6 sit on it, 5% apart.

Three vocabularies in all — steps, roles, surfaces — each with its own shape, and the shape says which one you are in.

Why prominence and not brightness

The tiers are named for how much they stand out, not for how light they are. A brightness word is true in one arm and false in the other: the dark ramp runs 0.664 to 0.970 while the light ramp runs 0.489 to 0.150, so brightest would resolve to near-black in light mode. Both arms land on the same contrast from opposite lightnesses, which is the property a brightness name would throw away.

subtle carries the same meaning here as it does in --itx-role-background-{{ "{" }}family{{ "}" }}-subtle — less prominent. One word, one meaning, two slots.

One cost of the optional segments: --itx-role-text-danger and --itx-role-text-subtle are the same shape but fill different slots, so position stops disambiguating. Both are small closed sets, so it is learnable rather than structural. Making family mandatory would fix it and cost length on the common case — neutral text is about 185 reads against seven for family text.

The page band is the first three rungs. At 2% they are +5/255 apart, which is where panel quality and ambient light decide whether the step exists at all. The cost of widening shows at the other end: the gap between the 7:1 tier and the far end falls from 0.127 to 0.075, so text-2 and text-3 converge.

Spacing tracks how often two members of a band sit side by side. Page levels rarely do, so they sit 3% apart. Layers sometimes do, so 5%. Text tiers are not spaced at all — each one is placed at the lightness that clears its floor against the deepest layer.

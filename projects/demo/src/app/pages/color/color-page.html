Two axes, and nothing in between. A component declares that it is a layer — never which grey it is — and its surface, borders and text all follow from the depth it happens to sit at.

Elevation is the substrate you stand on. Contrast is everything drawn on top of it. They are different kinds of thing, so they are written differently: elevation is always a spatial word, contrast is always a bare scalar. You cannot spell one in the other's notation.

Elevation

--itx-surface --itx-surface-above --itx-surface-above-2 --itx-surface-below

Moves toward light in both schemes. The light page is a mid-grey climbing to white; the dark page is near-black climbing to charcoal. Backgrounds only.

Contrast

--itx-contrast-1 … --itx-contrast-6 --itx-contrast-<family>-N

Moves away from the current surface, so its direction flips per scheme — darker in light, lighter in dark. A rank is a contrast target against whatever it sits on, not a fixed grey, which is why it stays correct at every depth and survives a hue change.

A palette, and then a verdict. Steps are placed by lightness alone — nothing here is positioned by a contrast target — and the guidance underneath was measured off the result. Nothing is a token; the generator behind it writes no CSS. The system above this section ships.

Even in OKLCH lightness is not even to the eye. On a linear ramp the last step removes 2.86× the light of the one before it while the first removes 1.26× — so it measures uniform and reads as accelerating into the dark. That is why neither Radix nor Tailwind is linear in any single space. Curve shrinks the lightness step toward the dark end to compensate; evenness below is the largest adjacent jump over the smallest, where 1.0 would be perfect.

Interop uses tone to say direction: a card is lighter than the page, a field darker. That is why the light page sits at mid-grey — it needs headroom both ways. No other system does this. Radix, Material and Carbon all move one way from the page and let shadow say which way.

Both frames below are pinned to light mode so they can be compared whatever scheme you are reading in, and both are hardcoded values rather than tokens — the right-hand one is a proposal the shipping system cannot currently express. Dark mode is unchanged either way; it already moves one direction.

The test is the pair at the bottom of each card. On the left they are distinguishable by tone alone, which is the mechanism working — and it costs a grey page. On the right they are the same tone and told apart by shadow, which is what every reference system does, and the page is white.

The rules precede the componentry, so components cohere without agreeing on anything. That must never make overriding harder — every library selector is zero-specificity and sits inside the interop cascade layer, so any rule you write wins on contact.

Override through a component's own namespace (--itx-dialog-background), which no layer block ever touches. The generic --itx-surface and --itx-contrast-* are written by the engine at every boundary, so setting those on an ancestor is overwritten one layer down — by design, and asserted in the spec so the reason stays visible.

To move the whole palette instead, set --itx-tint-light / --itx-tint-dark (chroma and hue) or any ramp dial — --itx-ramp-light-step, -page, -ease, -down, -min, -max, and the dark pair — on any ancestor. Surfaces are computed from those six numbers rather than tabulated per layer, so one dial retunes the entire ladder below it at once, live, with no regeneration.

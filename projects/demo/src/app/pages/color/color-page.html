Two axes, and nothing in between. A component declares that it is a layer and its surface follows from the depth it happens to sit at; what it draws on that surface it names from a palette of fixed steps.

Elevation is the substrate you stand on. The palette is everything drawn on top of it. They are different kinds of thing, so they are written differently: elevation is always a spatial word, a palette step is always a family and a number. You cannot spell one in the other's notation.

Elevation

Moves away from the page in both schemes, because tone carries distance and shadow carries direction. The light page is near-white and nests darker; the dark page is near-black and nests lighter. Backgrounds only.

The label on each fill is derived, not chosen, and the ratio under each one is measured — read off the rendered swatch in the browser, not copied from the generator. Cream Can comes out with dark text because white on it measures about 1.7:1, a correction that used to live in a comment and had to be remembered by whoever added the next hue.

A seed's chroma is an intent, not a promise. Every hue has its own ceiling in sRGB, and the arc below the line is where a default-strength seed gets clamped — which is why a lightness-indexed slot ramp could not survive a hue change, and a contrast target can.

A state is not a lighter or a darker version of the fill — it is a step away from the label, so a state can only ever improve the contrast the solver proved for the rest state. Which means the direction is not a constant. Jelly Bean carries a light label and darkens; Cream Can carries a dark label and lightens. Same rule, opposite result.

The rules precede the componentry, so components cohere without agreeing on anything. That must never make overriding harder — every library selector is zero-specificity and sits inside the interop cascade layer, so any rule you write wins on contact.

Override through a component's own namespace (--itx-dialog-background), which no layer block ever touches. The generic --itx-surface is written by the engine at every boundary, so setting it on an ancestor is overwritten one layer down — by design, and asserted in the spec so the reason stays visible. Palette steps are declared once at the root and are safe to override anywhere.

To move the whole palette instead, set --itx-tint-light / --itx-tint-dark (chroma and hue) or any ramp dial — --itx-ramp-light-step, -page, -ease, -down, -min, -max, and the dark pair — on any ancestor. Surfaces are computed from those six numbers rather than tabulated per layer, so one dial retunes the entire ladder below it at once, live, with no regeneration.
